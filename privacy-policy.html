Privacy Policy

Last updated: 7 August 2026

This is the privacy policy for StudyApp, an iPhone app for tracking study sessions and sharing them with friends.

We have written it to be read, not to be survived. If anything here is unclear, email andersrodeik@gmail.com and we will explain it in plain language.

Who is responsible for your data

Anders Roed, a private individual in Norway, is the data controller for the information described here.

Contact for anything privacy-related: andersrodeik@gmail.com

We do not have a Data Protection Officer. We are not required to appoint one, and the email above reaches a person, not a queue.

The short version

  • Your study sessions, grades and photos are private to you by default. Friends cannot read them.
  • When you share a session, we copy a small summary of it into a post. Your friends see that copy — they never gain access to the underlying data.
  • Everything is stored in the European Union (Sweden).
  • There is no advertising, no analytics, no tracking, and nothing is sold or shared with anyone outside what is described here.
  • You can delete your whole account from inside the app, and it really deletes — including your photo files. A deleted photo stops being served within about two minutes.
  • The app works offline. Signing in is optional; it is what turns on syncing and friends.

What we collect

We only collect what you type, tap, or attach. There is no background collection, no location tracking, and no monitoring of what you do on your phone.

Study sessions

  • Session mode (flow or deadline), start and end timestamps
  • Your goal length, if you set one
  • Calculated focused time and total elapsed time
  • Whether the session hit the 8-hour ceiling or was trimmed to its deadline
  • Whether notification permission was granted when the session started (so the app can warn you if reminders are silently off)
  • Pauses: a paused-at and resumed-at timestamp for each pause
  • Distractions: a timestamp for each time you tapped the distraction button. This is a manual tap and nothing more — see What we cannot see, below.
  • Focus rating: your own 1-to-5 rating of a finished session

Courses and grades

  • Term label and optional start/end dates
  • Course title and optional course code
  • Grades: the value exactly as you type it ("A", "Pass", "4"), the grading scale you chose, and an optional normalised number used to draw the effort-versus-outcome chart

Grades are self-reported. We do not connect to any school system, and we do not verify them.

Photos

You can attach one photo to a finished session.

  • The image is re-encoded on your device before it is stored or uploaded — decoded to pixels and written out as a fresh JPEG, capped at 1600 pixels on the long edge.
  • The re-encoded image carries no identifying EXIF: no GPS coordinates, no device or camera model, no lens information, no capture timestamp. To be exact rather than flattering: a small Exif block survives, containing image dimensions and resolution only. We are not going to claim "all EXIF removed", because that would not be true.
  • If the original photo was rotated, that rotation is baked into the pixels — because we strip the tag that would otherwise describe it.
  • We store the image file, its dimensions, its storage location, and when it was uploaded.
  • The app never gets access to your photo library. The picker runs outside the app, in the system, and hands us only the single image you chose. That is why the app never asks for photo library permission.

Friends and sharing

  • Friendships: who sent the request, who received it, whether it is pending, accepted or declined, and when. A copy of each person's display name is frozen onto the request, so a pending request can show who it is from without exposing anyone's profile.
  • Posts: whether the post is a session, a grade or a photo; whether it is visible to friends or kept private; any caption you type; and a snapshot of what you shared.

The snapshot is the important part, so here is exactly what it contains:

  • For a session: focused seconds, the session's start timestamp, the course name as plain text, and your focus score with its scale (e.g. "4 of 5").
  • For a grade: the grade as displayed ("A"), the scale's name ("A–F"), and the course name as plain text.

The snapshot deliberately does not contain the normalised numeric grade, the course ID, or the scale ID. It carries only what a friend's screen needs to draw the card.

This version of the app has no likes and no comments on posts.

Moderation

  • Blocks: who you blocked and when. Only you hold that record — the person you blocked is never told and never sees it.
  • Reports: who reported, what was reported (a post) and its ID, one of five fixed reasons, and whether it is open, actioned or dismissed. There is no free-text field in a report — deliberately, so that a moderation table cannot become a place where people write about each other.

Technical information

  • On your phone: the app writes diagnostic log lines using Apple's standard logging. They stay on your device. They are not sent to us.
  • On our servers: our hosting provider keeps ordinary web-server request logs for operating and securing the service. These include IP addresses and timestamps. We do not use them to build a profile of you, and we do not link them to your study data.
  • Via TestFlight: if you are on the beta and send feedback or a crash report, that goes to Apple and then to us under Apple's own terms. We do not receive it through the app.

What we cannot see

This matters enough to state plainly, because study apps are often assumed to do more:

  • We cannot see which apps you use or open. The app has no app-usage monitoring of any kind. Distraction records exist only because you tapped a button.
  • We cannot see your location. The app never requests it, and photo coordinates are stripped before the image is stored.
  • We cannot see your photo library. Only the single image you pick is ever handed to the app.
  • We do not read your school's systems. Grades are what you typed.

Where your data is stored

On your iPhone. The app is local-first: a database inside the app's own private container is the source of truth, along with any photo files. The timer, session logging, courses and grades all work fully offline, with no account.

On our servers, if you sign in. Signing in turns on syncing (so your data survives a reinstall or a new phone) and friends. Our backend is Supabase, hosted in the EU (Stockholm, Sweden). Your rows and your photo files live there and are not transferred outside the EEA by us.

Apple is a separate matter and a separate controller: distribution through TestFlight and the App Store, and any feedback you send through TestFlight, are governed by Apple's own privacy policy.

How your data is protected

  • In transit: everything travels over TLS.
  • Between users: every read of another person's data is checked inside the database by Row-Level Security policies, not by the app. The rule is: you can read a post only if you are an accepted friend of the author, the post is not private, and neither of you has blocked the other. Because it is enforced in the database, a bug in the app cannot expose someone else's data. We keep an automated test suite whose specific job is to assert that one account cannot read another account's private rows, and we run it against the real production database whenever those rules change.
  • Sessions, grades and photos are owner-only. Your friends cannot read those tables at all — not "we filter them out in the app", but "the database refuses". Sharing works by copying a snapshot into a post, which is why the snapshot is deliberately small.
  • Photos live in a private storage bucket. They are never public and never served from a permanent link. A friend viewing your shared photo gets a short-lived signed URL, minted only after the same friendship and visibility check passes. That check runs at the moment the link is asked for, so someone who unfriends you, gets blocked, or has a post hidden from them cannot get a new link at all — we have measured this against the live service. Once a link has been used, though, the image passes through a cache on its way to the phone; see How long we keep things for what that means when a photo is deleted.
  • At rest: our hosting provider encrypts its disks. We want to be honest about what that is worth: disk encryption protects against someone stealing a physical drive, and does not protect against a bad access rule. Grades are not encrypted field-by-field in this version. That is a deliberate, recorded decision, and the control we actually rely on is the read-path enforcement described above. We will revisit field-level encryption before we build any cross-user statistics feature.

We are not going to tell you the app is unhackable. What we can tell you is which specific control stands between one student and another student's grades, and that we test it.

How long we keep things

  • Your own data stays until you delete it or delete your account.
  • When you delete your account, your profile is anonymised and your sessions, pauses, distractions, focus ratings, courses, terms, grades, posts and photo files are deleted straight away — with one qualification about how quickly photos stop being served, in the next point. A scheduled server job then finishes anything the app could not — including removing your login record, which is the step the app itself is not permitted to do. In normal operation this completes within about 75 minutes; our commitment is within 24 hours.
  • A deleted photo stops being served within about two minutes. This is the one place in the app where "deleted" is not quite instant, so we would rather be precise about it than let you find out later. When a photo goes — because you deleted the session it belonged to, or your whole account, or we removed it after a report — the image file is deleted from our storage and its record is deleted from our database immediately, and from that moment nobody can be given access to it again. But images are delivered to phones through a caching network that sits in front of our servers, and a copy that somebody had already opened can keep loading from that cache for a short time afterwards. We measured this against the live service on 7 August 2026: the longest a removed photo kept being served was 102 seconds. Two things that window does not do: it does not let anyone new reach the image, and it does not apply to anyone who had not already opened it.
  • Reports are kept after an account is deleted. If you reported someone, or were reported, that record survives deletion. We keep it because a repeat-abuse pattern that vanishes when the account is deleted is not a moderation system. This is retained under Art. 17(3)(e) of the GDPR (establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims) and our legitimate interest in a safe service.
  • Blocks made against you are kept, because they belong to the person who made them, not to you.
  • Data on your own phone is not deleted when you sign out. This is deliberate: signing out is not the same as deleting, and wiping the device would destroy work you did offline that has not yet synced. To remove the local copy, delete the app. To remove everything everywhere, use Delete account.
  • Server request logs are kept by our hosting provider on their own retention schedule, on the order of weeks.

Sharing: what your friends see, and when

Because this is the part people get wrong about social apps, here it is exactly:

  1. When you finish a session, a summary screen appears. It lets you add a comment, a photo, a focus rating, and any distractions you forgot to log.
  2. Saving that screen publishes a post to your profile, where your accepted friends can see it. There is no separate "share" button, and that is by design — it is how the app works, the same way a run appears on a running app.
  3. If you tick "keep private", the post is saved but marked private. It is stored on our server and only you can see it.
  4. If you simply dismiss the screen without saving, no post is created at all.
  5. The rating, the distraction taps and the photo are saved to your own session as soon as you add them — they exist on your device (and sync to your private rows) whether or not you go on to post.
  6. Not sharing at all is also an option: if you never add friends, nothing you write is visible to anyone.

Your friends only ever see the snapshot described earlier. They never gain read access to your sessions, your grades, your courses or your photo records.

Children and young people

The app is built for students, and many of them are teenagers.

You must be at least 13 to use StudyApp. In Norway, 13 is the age at which a young person can agree to use an online service on their own account (Personal Data Act § 5). If you are under 18, we would encourage you to read this page with a parent — particularly the section on sharing.

We do not knowingly collect data from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has an account, email andersrodeik@gmail.com and we will delete it.

Automated decision-making

There is none. Nothing about your account, your grades or your visibility is decided by an algorithm. Moderation decisions — removing content, suspending an account — are made by a person.
